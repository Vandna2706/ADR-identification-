Task 3 - Causality Assessment
**Assessment 1: Aspirin & Heartburn/Nausea**
Temporal Relationship:Yes. Heartburn and nausea occurred during the 2-month timeline of drug administration.
Dechallenge: Unknown
Alternative Causes: Possible. The patient has a background medical history of a cardiovascular disorder, which may involve other medications or underlying physiological factors causing gastric distress.
Causality Classification:Possible
    Reason- The symptoms are a well-known side effect of Aspirin and match the timeline, but without clear dechallenge data and considering potential underlying factors, it remains a possible link.

**Assessment 2: Amoxicillin & Acute Generalized Rash**
Temporal Relationship: Yes. Strong temporal correlation as the patient developed a generalized rash, itching, and urticaria just 45 minutes after taking the drug.
Dechallenge: Yes. The patient fully recovered after drug discontinuation (and administration of an antihistamine).
Alternative Causes: Highly Unlikely. The rapid onset of systemic allergic symptoms (urticaria, itching) is a classic immediate hypersensitivity reaction to a beta-lactam antibiotic like Amoxicillin. Concomitant paracetamol was taken only on an "as needed" (SOS) basis.
Causality Classification: Certain or Probable 
    Reason- There is clear evidence of a strong chronological sequence, a positive recovery upon stopping the medication, and the clinical presentation perfectly matches an immunologic reaction to the drug.
